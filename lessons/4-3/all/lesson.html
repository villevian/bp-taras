Option A

1What topics do Beata and Mateo talk about?

Family and Mateo’s previous trips to Poland (Warsaw).

2How does Beata try to turn the discussion towards the Diabsensor?

She tries to change the subject at every chance she gets: ‘Can we move to the Diabsensor?’, ‘Is this a good moment to start talking about …?’, ‘I really think we should get to …’

3How does Mateo react to these attempts?

He ignores them or tells her they will discuss the Diabsensor later.

She does not manage to get Mateo to talk about the Diabsensor, and Mateo seems a little unhappy.

Option B

▶ 4.3.3 Option B – Beata goes with the flow open on YouTube

1What topics do Beata and Mateo talk about?

Family and Mateo’s previous trips to Poland (Warsaw).

2How does Beata show an interest in what Mateo is saying?

She answers his questions and asks her own (‘You sound like you know Warsaw well.’, ‘Of course.’).

3Does Beata try to turn the discussion towards the Diabsensor?

No, she doesn’t. She goes with the flow and waits for Mateo to turn the discussion towards it.

4Alex warned Beata that Mateo likes to multitask. What example of this do we see?

He takes a phone call during the meeting.

5Overall, how well does Beata manage the conversation?

She goes with the flow. She doesn’t talk about the Diabsensor, but the relationship stays friendly.
Discussion

4In pairs, discuss what you have learnt from Beata’s experience about adapting your communication style.

1What is the effect of going with the flow?

Beata doesn’t get to discuss her agenda, but she might build a better relationship with Mateo.

2What is the effect of focusing on the task in hand?

Beata’s attempts to bring the conversation back to the Diabsensor affect the relationship: Mateo is less friendly in Option A than in Option B.

Pronunciation bank: -(e)s endings

We add an extra syllable /ɪz/ after the sounds /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/: go – goes (1 syllable) but age – ag·es (2 syllables).

TASK

10A Work in pairs. Read the situation and decide who will be the supplier (S) and who will be the client (C). Read your instructions.

A supplier is visiting a client to talk about a new app which his/her company has just produced. This app will be very useful for managing a database of business contacts, and is easy to install, simple to use and inexpensive.
Student S – Supplier
Your company has just produced a new app for managing a database of business contacts. Your aim is to present it and agree on a trial.
  • Key points: easy to install (10 minutes), simple to use, works on phone and laptop, syncs with Outlook and Google Contacts.
  • Price: €4 per user per month; 30-day free trial.
  • You don’t have much time: you have another meeting in 40 minutes. Try to stay on track politely.
Student C – Client
You are interested in learning about the app, but that will be later. First you want to talk about other things:
  • the supplier’s journey and the new office you have just moved to;
  • a conference you both went to last year;
  • a problem with your current system (your sales team lost 200 contacts last month).
  • When you finally talk about the app, ask about price, data security and training for your team.

B Hold your meeting. Try to change the subject and stay on track as necessary, using phrases from Exercise 7.

C When you have finished, discuss how the meeting went. Was it successful? What was easy and what was difficult? What could you do better?

D Find a new partner, change roles and repeat the conversation.
